# Social content plan

Prepared 19 August 2026 for the two public accounts:

- `@nxth.b` — cybersecurity research, software building and BstudioB
- `@n.brown02` — spoken word, music, performance and public storytelling

The accounts should remain separate. They can cross-reference one another, but each profile should answer a different question within three seconds.

## Publishing rhythm

Use a lightweight four-week cycle rather than trying to post every day:

- Two reels per week (one useful/educational, one human/process-led).
- One carousel or photo post per week.
- Stories on recording, research, events and works-in-progress.
- Reuse each strong reel as a YouTube Short and a website artefact where appropriate.

## @nxth.b — technical sequence

### Week 1 — Start here

**Reel:** “What I research and build” — direct-to-camera, 20–30 seconds. Explain that you turn difficult safety questions into usable systems and evidence.

**Carousel:** “Three things I build” — research evidence, practical software, safer operational workflows. Use one clear screenshot per slide.

### Week 2 — Show the work

**Reel:** Device Provisioning Toolkit walkthrough. Open with the decision problem, show the comparison flow, then state the outcome.

**Carousel:** Inspector or FlowCue case study: problem → approach → working result → what you learned.

### Week 3 — Research to product

**Reel:** MyMark concept explained at a high level: decentralised verification against abuse and sextortion. Keep implementation details private.

**Carousel:** “From research question to useful product” using the EPQ, undergraduate dissertation and PGDip work as a short timeline.

### Week 4 — Professional proof

**Reel:** OSINT / security event insight: one idea you heard, why it matters and how it changes your practice.

**Carousel:** “What I’m building through BstudioB” — product discovery, pilots and partnerships, with a clear BstudioB call to action.

**Technical caption pattern:**

> The problem: [one sentence].  
> What I tested: [one sentence].  
> What changed: [one sentence].  
> Follow for research notes and working systems.

## @n.brown02 — creative sequence

### Week 1 — Reintroduce the voice

**Reel:** “I write real stories.” A 15–25 second spoken-word introduction with captions and a clean, consistent cover.

**Carousel:** Three stills from a performance with one short line of context, collaborator credits and a link to the full work.

### Week 2 — Live work

**Reel:** Strongest performance excerpt, beginning with the most immediate line rather than an introduction.

**Photo/reel:** Behind-the-scenes rehearsal, soundcheck or staging moment. Keep the caption personal and specific.

### Week 3 — Music and making

**Reel:** Producer / piano / vocal process clip. Show the change from rough idea to finished moment.

**Carousel:** A visual diary from *Again, Again & Again* or another performance, with collaborators tagged once and credits kept readable.

### Week 4 — Public conversation

**Reel:** BBC interview excerpt or a short reflection on the issue behind a piece.

**Photo/reel:** Croydonites or booking proof with a clear “watch / book / follow” call to action.

**Creative caption pattern:**

> A real moment from [work / place].  
> I wrote this because [specific reason].  
> Full piece / next performance: [link destination].

## Link architecture

The portfolio should eventually provide one lightweight link page with two routes:

- **Technical:** BstudioB, research, software demos and CV.
- **Creative:** performances, YouTube, BBC interview, Croydonites and contact.

BstudioB should be the technical account’s primary destination; it should not replace the creative account’s Croydonites-facing identity.

## Quality checklist

- Hook in the first two seconds of every reel.
- Burn in captions for spoken audio and technical explanations.
- Use one idea per post and one call to action.
- Credit collaborators clearly, without filling the caption with hashtags.
- Use consistent covers so each grid reads as a body of work.
- Keep personal milestones that support the story; archive only content that actively confuses the account’s purpose.
